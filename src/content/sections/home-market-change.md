## A market under structural pressure

The specialist magazine publishing sector, which forms the core of Esco's client base, has faced mounting pressure since 2022 — declining print circulation, growing complexity in managing audiences across print and digital simultaneously, and a fundamental shift in how publishers think about revenue.

- UK magazine publisher revenue is forecast to shrink at a **compound annual rate of 5.2%** ([IBISWorld](https://img.ibisworld.com/united-kingdom/industry/magazine-publishers/3475))
- **Print circulation fell 12.4%** in 2023 alone, accelerating pressure on fulfilment and technology partners
- By end of 2024, **55% of B2B publishing companies** had moved to subscription-based revenue models — a **12-point rise** since 2022 ([Global Growth Insights](https://www.globalgrowthinsights.com/market-reports/b2b-publishing-market-100130))

This shift from single-copy and print-first thinking to a recurring, audience-centric model requires more sophisticated data management and renewal logic than most legacy stacks were built to support.

## New competition for the bureau model

Alongside changes in publisher behaviour, the competitive landscape for subscription management has evolved. A new generation of SaaS platforms has entered the market, positioning themselves as modern alternatives to the traditional bureau model.

- **Pelcro** was founded in 2016 specifically out of frustration with the lack of truly modern magazine subscription management services ([Pelcro](https://www.get.pelcro.com/magazine-subscription-management-software)), and now offers self-serve subscription management, paywall control and billing automation in a single platform
- **Darwin CX**, a Toronto-based subscription and customer experience platform, has accelerated its push into the UK market — recently winning accounts including National Geographic Kids and The Chelsea Magazine Company, with publishers citing a need to consolidate fragmented systems and improve data visibility ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/darwin-cx-wins-new-global-publisher-accounts-as-media-companies-prioritize-unified-subscription-data-302761952.html))

These platforms compete on flexibility, real-time data access and lower perceived overhead. The pressure on bureau-model businesses like Esco is to demonstrate the value of managed services, deep institutional knowledge and the kind of hands-on client relationship that SaaS platforms do not typically provide.
